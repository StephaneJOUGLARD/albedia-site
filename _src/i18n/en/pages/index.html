The problem

Every summer, your roof costs you dearly

In full sun, a dark roof behaves like a radiator placed on top of your building. You pay for this heat in several ways, and a little more every summer.

85 to 95%

of solar radiation is absorbed by a dark traditional roof.

66 to 85 °C

on the surface of a traditional roof in high summer.

31 to 47 °C

above the ambient air, compared with 6 to 11 °C for a reflective roof.

  • {{icon:clim}}

    Air conditioning running flat out

    The hotter the roof, the more heat the air conditioning has to remove. In more than 25 studies reviewed by the US EPA, a reflective roof reduced air-conditioning consumption by 2 to 40%, and by 20% on average.

  • {{icon:euro}}

    Power peaks that weigh on the bill

    Electricity demand peaks during the hottest hours. The same studies found reductions of 14 to 38% in peak cooling demand with a cool roof.

  • {{icon:thermo}}

    Less effective teams

    Workshops, loading docks, sales floors: when heat builds up under the roof, comfort and alertness drop.

  • {{icon:couches}}

    A roof that wears out faster

    Heated every day and cooled at night, a dark roof covering undergoes large temperature swings that fatigue its materials.

Source: US EPA, Reducing Urban Heat Islands, chapter Cool Roofs. These are orders of magnitude from published studies: the actual gain depends on each building, which is what our preliminary study assesses. All sources

Climate change

Ever hotter summers: what the IPCC experts conclude

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) assesses the scientific knowledge on climate. The conclusions of its Sixth Assessment Report directly concern your buildings.

+1.1 °C

Warming is already here

Global surface temperature rose by about 1.1 °C between 1850-1900 and 2011-2020. For the IPCC, it is “unequivocal” that human influence has warmed the atmosphere, ocean and land.

1950

More frequent heatwaves since the 1950s

It is virtually certain that hot extremes, including heatwaves, have become more frequent and more intense across most land regions since the 1950s.

2.8 times

Heat peaks that come back more often

Over land, an extreme heat event that occurred once every ten years on average without human influence already happens 2.8 times more often, and it is about 1.2 °C hotter.

5.6 times

And the trend is growing

With 2 °C of global warming, such a heat peak would occur 5.6 times more often, and 9.4 times at 4 °C. Every additional increment of warming makes extremes more pronounced.

For your buildings, this means hotter summers and roofs that build up ever more heat. Making your roof reflective adapts your property to the coming climate, starting now.

The question we are asked

And in winter, will the building be colder?

A reflective roof also captures less sunlight in winter. But at that time of year the sun is low and the days are short: solar gains through the roof are small. In most of the climates studied, the slight increase in heating remains smaller than the summer savings.

The balance does, however, depend on the building. A study carried out for the French Building Federation found a very small effect when heating accounts for 75% of the needs, and a favourable balance as soon as air conditioning exceeds 30% of the needs. This is why we study your building before proposing anything.

The system

Three layers for a long-lasting roof

  1. 1

    The existing surface, prepared

    Cleaned, repaired and treated at roof details. It determines the adhesion and the lifespan of the coating.

  2. 2

    The primer

    It makes the coating adhere. Its type depends on the surface: metal, bitumen, synthetic membrane or concrete.

  3. 3

    The reflective coating

    Applied in one or more coats, it provides reflectance and emissivity. Its performance is measured by the SRI, when new and after ageing.

The number of coats and the products depend on the system chosen for your roof.

Durability

Lasting performance, if the coating is properly applied and maintained

Properly applied, many cool roof coatings have lasted more than twenty years. Conversely, a poorly executed application can lead to peeling and flaking within a few years.

Reflectance drops mainly in the first year, due to soiling: by about 20% with proper maintenance. Cleaning in line with the manufacturer’s recommendations helps to preserve it.

Which process?

Four families of reflective processes

Depending on the type of surface and its condition, several processes exist. The roof visit is used to choose the right one.

Reflective paints

Applied to a sound surface, they provide reflectance. They provide neither waterproofing nor insulation.

Liquid waterproofing systems

Resins applied on site that form both a waterproof layer and a light-coloured surface.

Reflective bituminous waterproofing

Bituminous membranes whose exposed face is light-coloured and reflective.

Reflective synthetic waterproofing

Light-coloured synthetic membranes, laid when the waterproofing is renewed.
