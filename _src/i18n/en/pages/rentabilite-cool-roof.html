Cool roof profitability and ROI

A reflective roof pays for itself through air-conditioning savings, any incentives, such as the CEE bonus in France, and a roof that heats up less. Here are the levers, the method for calculating the payback period and a calculator to estimate yours.

Profitability

Two indicators to judge a project

A cool roof is an investment: you pay for the works once, then every summer you recover part of the energy that the air conditioning no longer has to use. Two figures are enough to judge it.

The payback period shows how many years it takes for the savings to pay back the works, once any bonus has been deducted. It is the figure finance departments look at first.

The net gain measures what remains once the works have been paid back. We calculate it over 20 years, the conventional lifetime used by the French CEE sheet BAT-EN-112 to calculate the bonus. This is not a guaranteed lifespan: that depends on the product, the surface and maintenance.

Both rely on the same figure: the air-conditioning energy actually saved each year. This is what needs to be estimated carefully, building by building.

The formulas

Net investmentcost of the works − incentives (CEE bonus in France)

Payback periodnet investment ÷ annual savings

Net gain over 20 yearsannual savings × 20 − net investment

With no rise in energy prices and no discounting: a cautious assumption.

What pays

The savings levers of a reflective roof

The first two are easy to quantify. The others also weigh in the decision, even if they rarely appear in a spreadsheet.

{{icon:clim}}

Less air conditioning

This is the main gain. In more than 25 studies reviewed by the US EPA, a reflective roof reduced air-conditioning consumption by 2 to 40%, and by 20% on average.

{{icon:euro}}

Incentives

In France, for a tertiary building in commercial use that meets the conditions of sheet BAT-EN-112, the CEE bonus reduces the investment from the outset: 160 to 270 kWh cumac per m² of treated roof, depending on the climate zone. Other countries have their own rules.

{{icon:mesure}}

Lower power peaks

The same studies found reductions of 14 to 38% in peak cooling demand. Depending on your electricity contract, the part of the bill linked to capacity may benefit.

{{icon:thermo}}

More liveable premises

Less heat under the roof means better comfort for staff and customers. In France, since 1 July 2025, employers must prevent heat-related risks, in particular by adapting workplaces.

{{icon:couches}}

Less stress on the roof covering

A roof that heats up less undergoes smaller temperature swings between day and night. The works are also an opportunity to treat corrosion, cracks and roof details.

{{icon:bouclier}}

Energy-saving targets

In France, every kWh of cooling avoided counts towards the cut in consumption required of tertiary buildings of 1,000 m² and more: −40% in 2030 compared with the reference year.

What about winter? The roof captures a little less solar heat. According to the US EPA, this slight extra heating cost is generally offset by the summer savings, because the sun is lower and the days shorter. It weighs more in buildings that are mainly heated: this is one of the points our study checks. Find out more

Sources: US EPA, Cool Roofs; US EPA, Using Cool Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands; sheet BAT-EN-112; French decree no. 2025-482 of 27 May 2025 (our page, in French); French tertiary decree (in French).

Investment

What determines the cost of a cool roof

We do not publish a price per square metre: two roofs of the same area can require very different works. The quote is drawn up after visiting the roof, item by item. Here is what makes it vary.

  • {{icon:loupe}}The area. Site set-up, access and safety are spread over more square metres when the roof is large.
  • {{icon:loupe}}The surface and its condition. Cleaning, corrosion treatment, repair of cracks or blisters, replacement of damaged elements.
  • {{icon:loupe}}The system applied. Primer suited to the material, number of coats and thickness specified by the manufacturer.
  • {{icon:loupe}}Roof details. Upstands, rooflights, smoke vents, technical equipment and translucent sheets to be protected.
  • {{icon:loupe}}Access and safety. Building height, guardrails or nets, lifting equipment, fragile roof.
  • {{icon:loupe}}Site constraints. Activity maintained during the works, working hours, areas to be isolated.
  • {{icon:loupe}}Asbestos. On old fibre cement sheets: prior survey and specific working method.

Any incentive is then deducted when the project is eligible. In France, the CEE bonus must be applied for with a funder before the quote is signed: we check its conditions from the study stage.

Method

Calculating the return on investment in five steps

This is the approach of our study. The calculator below follows a simplified version of it.

  1. 1

    Start from the air-conditioning consumption

    The best starting point is your bills, your sub-meters or your building management system. Failing that, it is estimated from the average consumption of buildings with the same use and the share of air conditioning in it.

  2. 2

    Isolate what comes through the roof

    The roof only acts on the top storey. On a single-storey warehouse or shop, it covers the whole volume; on a four-storey office building, only a quarter. Insulation also counts: a roof that is already well insulated lets less heat through, and the gain is smaller.

  3. 3

    Apply a realistic gain

    The US EPA reports 20% savings on air conditioning on average, from 2 to 40% depending on the building. A study published in 2024 by the French Building Federation, based on simulations of French buildings, found a positive effect on cooling needs throughout France, while stressing that it depends heavily on use, insulation and location: the gain is calculated project by project.

  4. 4

    Value the kWh and deduct incentives

    The kWh saved are valued at the price in your contract. Benchmark: €0.25 excl. VAT per kWh on average for small business customers in France in the first half of 2026, according to Eurostat. The cost of the works is reduced by any incentive actually obtained, such as the CEE bonus in France.

  5. 5

    Calculate the payback period and the gain

    The net investment divided by the annual savings gives the payback period. The savings accumulated over 20 years, minus the net investment, give the gain. A low and a high range make it possible to decide knowing the uncertainty.

Sources: US EPA; French Building Federation, Quel est l’impact réel des procédés réflectifs (dits cool roof) en toiture ?, June 2024 (in French); Eurostat prices, quoted by Selectra (in French).

What counts
the heat under the roof

The benefit is measured in working conditions, in goods less exposed to heat, and sometimes in air conditioning that does not need to be installed.

These figures follow from the assumptions detailed under the calculator: they illustrate a method and are no substitute for a study. Outside France, there is no CEE bonus: in Italy or Spain, where the calculator assumes the same share of air conditioning as in zone H3, the shop in the first profile would save about €5,280 per year, i.e. a budget of about €52,800 paid back in 10 years. In summary

When is a cool roof most profitable?

{{icon:check}}Fastest payback

  • Single-storey buildings, where the roof covers the whole volume
  • Significant air conditioning, or processes and storage kept cool
  • Dark roofs
  • Hot climates and high kWh prices
  • Projects eligible for incentives, such as the CEE bonus in France

{{icon:loupe}}Worth a close look

  • Multi-storey buildings
  • Roofs that are already light-coloured
  • Roofs with little or no insulation, whose winter balance needs checking
  • Non-air-conditioned buildings that overheat in summer
  • Roofs to be renovated anyway: the coating is added to planned works

{{icon:fermer}}Hardly profitable

  • Mainly heated buildings, with no need for cooling
  • Roofs that are already white and reflective: cleaning may be enough
  • Surfaces that are too damaged and must be redone before any coating
